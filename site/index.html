Beyond Dodge Rate

Testing Humanoid Dodgeball Under Camera Failures

Kingston Kuan, Brandon Ong
Hebbian Robotics
Paper Supplement Code Video BibTeX

Abstract

A humanoid robot can dodge a ball and still leave its playing area. We study this gap under depth-camera failures, measuring contact, balance, and area compliance throughout each four-second trial. Two saved G1 policies, π₁ and π₂, use the same training method and recorded settings but respond differently to stale and blank images across 192 paired test seeds per policy. For π₁, all 122 area exits under stale images occur after the throw resolves. In a separate fixed paired recovery test with 64 seeds, restoring fresh images at two seconds increases success from 50.0% to 95.3%, while dodge rate remains 100%. Maximum excursion also decreases without an area cutoff. The same intervention does not improve overall success for π₂. Shared ball trajectories preserve the π₁ recovery benefit. These results support evaluating motion after an encounter and testing camera handling on the policy in use. They concern two fixed models in simulation and do not establish a universal camera fallback or hardware safety.

Results

A humanoid can dodge an incoming ball while moving outside its permitted area. We measure both contact avoidance and overall success: dodging, staying upright, and keeping its pelvis within a 1 m radius over four seconds.

In the fixed 64-seed recovery comparison, restoring fresh images at two seconds raises π₁ success from 32/64 to 61/64, with 64/64 dodges in both conditions. The improvement is 45.3 percentage points (95% paired interval +32.8 to +57.8). Mean maximum excursion falls by 18.4 cm. Overall success for π₂ changes from 53/64 to 51/64. These are comparisons within each saved policy.

Blank images improve area compliance for both models. For π₁, that benefit outweighs a small loss in dodges. For π₂, a larger loss in dodges outweighs the area benefit. We have not established why the learned models respond differently.

π₁ is the checkpoint released by Martino Russi; π₂ was trained by Hebbian Robotics using the same PPO method, architecture, objective, and recorded settings. The labels identify two saved policies. Results compare camera conditions within each policy; they do not rank the policies.

Evaluation protocol and limitations

For each policy, the robot starts from the same state and faces the same throw across camera conditions. The controller updates 50 times per second and receives 25 camera images per second. Over each four-second trial, we record whether it dodges, falls, or leaves the area. A successful trial must dodge, stay upright, and stay in the area. Aborted throws remain failures.

What the results cover

We tested two saved policies on one simulated robot. We did not test physical robot safety, human collaboration, or team play, and did not repeat training with different seeds. We knew when the camera interruption began; we did not train a failure detector.

Related Links

The simulation and training implementation originates from G1 Depth Dodge, adapting ViBe/ORCS. Training uses RSL-RL PPO in mjlab/MuJoCo Warp, with frozen SONIC and Theia bases. The standalone evaluation port uses LeRobot G1 assets.

Our team trained a separate depth adaptation with settings matching the released reference configuration: seed 42, 4,000 updates, and 512 environments. Our run used eight GPUs. Matching settings do not establish identical initialization, random-number sequences, or resume histories; those histories are incomplete. We evaluated camera failures on the two resulting sets of weights. The data package contains configurations and raw trial records; analysis code is in the repository.

We corrected an inactive-ball collision bug in the upstream deployment port. Collision masks are disabled while the ball is inactive and restored at launch; weights and active-throw physics remain unchanged. Earlier cohorts are corrected exploratory reanalyses; the checkpoint comparison uses 192 new held-out seeds.

The manuscript is unpublished. The repository contains evaluation and analysis code, configurations, and manuscript source. The four recorded-trial archives are prepared but have not been publicly released. Reference policy weights are available from their original release; the separately trained weights are not distributed. Public end-to-end reproduction is not currently available.
