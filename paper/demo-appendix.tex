\section{Demo: Camera Failures in Simulated Humanoid Dodgeball}
\label{app:demo}
\subsection{What viewers see}
The demo presents synchronized replays of the experiments in this paper.
Viewers compare camera conditions, pause at measured events, and follow the
robot's pelvis relative to the allowed area of radius 1\,m. The examples use the first declared
seeds; aggregate results are reported separately so that an illustrative trial
is not mistaken for average performance.

The overview video first compares healthy input with stale-input faults at ball
launch and 320\,ms later. In the illustrated trial, the later stale-input fault allows a
dodge but the robot subsequently leaves the area. The video then compares
fresh-image restoration, followed by healthy, stale, and blank input for
$\pi_2$. The opening slide defines $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ and identifies their sources;
separate tables report the 64-seed original restoration experiment and the 192-seed input
comparison. These are two sets of learned weights trained with the same method
and recorded settings, not two proposed training methods.

The recovery replay compares continuous stale input with fresh
frames restored at two or three seconds. All three robots dodge the same throw;
only the two-second restoration stays within the area in this example. The
three-second example is labeled exploratory. The
paper's aggregate restoration results, rather than this single replay, support
the reported effect.

\subsection{Playback and setup}
The videos use aligned views and synchronized holds so that the encounter and
later movement can be compared. An orange marker remains briefly after the
ball's native removal to make its last recorded position visible. The marker
is a display aid, not a simulated rebound or continued ball physics.

The demo consists of recorded simulation, rather than live policy execution.
A laptop with a browser is sufficient for presentation. The presenters bring
the laptop; a power outlet and an optional external display are useful.
Downloaded videos play offline. No physical robot, GPU, or special floor area
is needed. Online playback of the YouTube version requires internet access.

\subsection{Video}
The overview video is available at \url{https://youtu.be/iXgu4AaxdYE}.
Synchronized replays are available on the project
website linked below the paper title.

The results concern two fixed policies in one simulated G1 task. They do not
establish hardware safety or variation across independent training runs.

\subsection{Code, data and weight availability}
Evaluation code, original configurations, dependency locks, and provenance are
available through the project website. Five recorded-trial archives
are publicly released at
\url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/hebbianrobotics/beyond-dodge-rate}.
They cover the original 2,496-trial study, the 800 pilot and original restoration
trials, the 660 shared-throw and preflight trials, the 128 later-restoration
trials, and the 384 stale-to-blank control trials. Repository manifests specify
archive and file hashes.

The released reference weights are available at
\url{https://huggingface.co/nepyope/g1_depth_dodge}.
The evaluated $\pi_2$ actor and perception ONNX files are public at
\url{https://huggingface.co/hebbianrobotics/g1-depth-dodge}.
The shared encoders come from the reference release. The retained $\pi_2$
training checkpoint is not distributed. Fresh simulation requires the pinned
simulator, robot assets, and applicable model files under their original
terms. Recomputing the original statistics requires the trial archives.
Cross-runtime exact replay remains a separate qualification step.

The stale-to-blank archive's hash and its record hashes are in
\path{data/stale-to-blank-manifest.json}; the archive is named
\path{stale-to-blank-data-v1.tar.gz}. It contains each condition's configuration,
frame-hash ledger, physical traces, native outcomes, and execution metadata.
Reference summaries and per-seed outcomes are in
\path{results/stale-to-blank-v1/}.

\subsection{Reproducing the stale-to-blank analysis}
\begingroup\raggedright
The repository includes \path{scripts/analyze_stale_to_blank.py}, its helper
modules, both frozen configurations under \path{configs/}, the data manifests,
and locked dependencies. Use Python 3.12 and \texttt{uv}. Download
\path{camera-recovery-data-v1.tar.gz} and
\path{stale-to-blank-data-v1.tar.gz} from the dataset above into
\path{data/}. The latter archive is required for reproducing the retained
control results. Run the following from the
repository root; the trial and output directories must not already exist.
\begingroup\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
uv sync --locked
mkdir -p outputs/stale-to-blank-records
tar -xzf data/stale-to-blank-data-v1.tar.gz \
  -C outputs/stale-to-blank-records
uv run python scripts/analyze_stale_to_blank.py \
  --trials outputs/stale-to-blank-records \
  --recovery-data \
    data/camera-recovery-data-v1.tar.gz \
  --output outputs/stale-to-blank-analysis
\end{verbatim}
\endgroup
The command verifies the recovery archive and its file hashes, recomputes all
384 control outcomes, checks all 128 matched triplets and their pre-switch
camera and physical histories, and repeats the 10,000-resample paired
bootstrap. It writes \path{summary.json} and \path{trials.csv} under
\path{outputs/stale-to-blank-analysis/}; these should match the supplied files in
\path{results/stale-to-blank-v1/}. It also generates the supplementary
\path{switch-table.tex}. The original archive supplies the separate historical
comparison, not pooled observations. This recorded-data analysis needs no
weights, simulator execution, or GPU and does not assert exact cross-runtime
replay. To run fresh simulations, use \path{scripts/evaluate.py} with
\path{configs/stale-to-blank-upstream-v1.json} and
\path{configs/stale-to-blank-team-v1.json} and the corresponding corrected
simulator inputs and weights. The repository's reproduction guide gives the
commands; fresh runs remain separate from the retained results.
