\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{app:protocol}
This supplement accompanies \emph{Beyond Dodge Rate: Testing Humanoid Dodgeball
Under Camera Failures}. Section A gives reproducibility details, Section B
reports supporting results, and Section C describes the demo and releases.

\subsection{Policies, simulator, and task}
The controller combines frozen SONIC and Theia bases with depth adaptation
trained by PPO through RSL-RL in mjlab/MuJoCo Warp, using the sources cited in
the main paper. $\pi_1$ is the released iteration-4000 model. The saved
configuration for $\pi_2$ matches the released reference: training seed 42,
4,000 updates, 512 environments, and about 49.15 million transitions,
distributed across eight GPUs in our run. The weights differ. Initialization,
random-number sequences, and resume histories are incomplete, so these are
not a controlled comparison of training methods or independent training
replications with fully controlled histories.

Evaluation fixes weights, native gains, torque limits, depth noise,
active-ball collisions, and fall checks. MuJoCo 3.3.7 and ONNX Runtime 1.23.2
run on CPU, with 50 Hz control and 25 Hz depth frames. All trials correct the
inactive-ball bug by disabling collisions while the ball is parked below the
floor and restoring them at launch.

The ball launches at 1 s. The native generator aims at one of six body
locations, adds random position offsets and $\pm30^\circ$ direction variation,
and uses a nominal flight time of 0.55--0.62 s. Trials end at 4 s or on a fall.
The allowed area is a circle of radius 1 m centred on the pelvis starting
position. A fall occurs when pelvis height is below 0.45 m or upright-axis
cosine is below 0.4 (about $66.4^\circ$ tilt).

\subsection{Cohorts and fixed comparisons}
The input-handling experiment fixes 192 seeds (7300--7491) per policy before
observing outcomes. Each seed runs healthy input, continuous stale input, and
continuous blank input: 1,152 trials across both policies. Fault onset is
160 ms after launch (1.16 s), and faults last until trial end.

The original fresh-image restoration experiment uses a separate exploratory
16-seed pilot per policy (8000--8015), then 64 new seeds (9000--9063). Each seed
runs five conditions: healthy input, continuous stale input, continuous blank
input, stale input followed by fresh-image restoration at 2 s, and blank input
followed by restoration at 2 s. Faults begin at 1.16 s (160 ms after launch).
This gives 160 pilot and 640 original restoration trials. After the pilot,
we fixed the new seeds and the primary contrast:
restoration versus continuous stale input for $\pi_1$. The other policy and
blank-input contrasts are secondary. Pilot outcomes are excluded.

Restoration follows a fixed schedule rather than being triggered by throw
resolution. All conditions retain the four-second horizon, subject to the
native fall termination rule, without further outcome-dependent stopping.
Histories are not reset. All stale-input throws resolve by restoration within
numerical tolerance. The original 2,496-trial package also includes exploratory
cohorts beyond the 192-seed input-handling experiment. Exploratory controls and
post-hoc scoring analyses are not pooled into the original restoration estimate.

\subsection{Matching and shared ball launches}
Within each policy and seed, prelaunch robot positions and velocities, launch
time, target, ball position and velocity, and flight time must match across
camera conditions. Camera schedules are checked. Missing or mismatched trials
halt analysis. Restoration pairs also have identical recorded physical samples
before 2 s. Different robot motion before launch can change the simulator's
aim, so equal seeds need not yield equal throws across policies. Comparisons
concern camera inputs within each policy rather than a policy ranking.

The shared-throw control tests whether responses persist under matched ball
launches. For index $i=0,\ldots,63$, donor seed $9000+i$ supplies a healthy-input
throw from $\pi_1$ when $i$ is even and $\pi_2$ when $i$ is odd. This selects
32 launches per policy without filtering outcomes. Simulation seeds
10000--10063 apply each launch to both policies under all five restoration
conditions, with the same 1.16 s fault onset and 2 s restoration schedule,
giving 640 trials. Twenty separate preflight trials use two other
simulation seeds and are retained in the shared-throw archive.

At launch, the evaluator retains native collision settings, outcome accounting,
and random draws, then replaces only the ball's initial position and velocity
before integration. Exact throw parameters match across all ten
policy-condition combinations per seed. Robot states can still differ. The
target label describes the donor's original aim, not the receiving robot's body
part. Subsequent ball trajectories are simulated, not imposed. This control
reuses recorded launch conditions and adds no independent training runs.

\subsection{Scoring, timing, and uncertainty}
Task success requires a dodge, no fall, and area compliance throughout the
trial. A dodge is a simulator-recorded miss. Trials ending before throw
resolution (aborted throws) count as failures and remain in the denominator.
Area compliance fails at the first sampled exit, even if the robot returns.
The area criterion measures pelvis containment, not whole-body containment or
damage, and is neither a court rule nor an injury threshold.

The post-hoc timing analysis reuses all 1,152 input-handling trials without
changing seeds or outcome labels. A hit resolves at native contact. A miss
resolves at the first 20 ms control step at least one second after launch if
the ball remains active after contact processing and the fall check. Resolution
is a scoring event, not a measurement of closest approach. An area exit is late
only if it occurs strictly after resolution; ties count as at-or-before
resolution. Area exits use 50 Hz samples without interpolation.

Paired 95\% confidence intervals use 10,000 bootstrap resamples of whole
evaluation seeds, retaining their conditions together. Shared-throw comparisons
retain both policies in each resampled seed. The input-handling, original
restoration, shared-throw input-effect, and stale-to-blank analyses use random
seed 20261014. The area-radius/duration analysis uses 20261015, and its
continuous-distance analysis uses 20261016. Intervals are nominal per comparison,
without multiplicity adjustment. Individual-condition intervals are Wilson
intervals. Evaluation seeds are not independent training replications.

The trial archives retain configurations, camera schedules, physical traces,
and outcome flags. Analysis code computes intervals and validates matching
from these records. These records do not by themselves imply identical replay
across different execution environments.

\subsection{Controller and station reward}
Each 25 Hz depth frame is encoded by Theia. A perception module aggregates
16 recent visual tokens into a feature. At 50 Hz, the actor combines this
feature with a standing reference and ten samples each of gyroscope readings,
joint-position offsets from the default pose, joint velocities, previous
actions, and projected gravity. The standing reference includes default joint
positions, zero joint velocities, and orientation relative to the reference.
The 29 normalized outputs specify joint targets through native scaling,
PD gains, and torque limits.

Fault injection replaces depth values before preprocessing and Theia encoding.
Visual and proprioceptive histories keep updating through switches. Old visual
tokens remain until newer tokens replace them. Blank input supplies zero-valued
depth frames at the native camera rate. Fresh-image restoration resumes the
native noisy camera stream.

The pinned training environment inherits the station term
\begin{equation}
r_{\rm station}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\|\mathbf p_{\rm ref}
-\mathbf p_{\rm robot}\|_2^2}{\sigma^2}\right).
\end{equation}
The weight is $w=0.3$ and the scale is $\sigma=1$ m. The positions are the
reference and robot anchor positions in world coordinates, including height.
The reward manager applies the weight and its time-step scaling. The term is
\texttt{motion\_global\_anchor\_position\_error\_exp} in
\href{https://github.com/mujocolab/mjlab/blob/v1.4.0/src/mjlab/tasks/tracking/mdp/rewards.py}{mjlab 1.4.0}.
The environment also rewards ball clearance and penalizes action changes and
joint-limit violations. The depth-adaptation wrapper adds hit and fall
penalties. The horizontal, trial-wide area criterion is additional to this
smooth reward. Its 1 m scale supplies no task-level justification for a hard
area radius. Training hyperparameters were fixed, so their sensitivity is
outside these tests.

\section{Detailed Results}
\label{app:results}
\subsection{Input handling and late area exits}
Blank versus stale input raises $\pi_1$ success from 70/192 to 171/192
(+52.6 percentage points; paired 95\% confidence interval +43.8 to +60.9).
For $\pi_2$, success falls from 158/192 to 100/192 ($-$30.2 percentage points;
confidence interval $-$37.0 to $-$23.4). For $\pi_1$, area compliance rises
from 70/192 to 181/192 while dodges fall from 191/192 to 182/192. For $\pi_2$,
area compliance rises from 182/192 to 190/192 while dodges fall from 165/192 to
101/192. There are no falls. All conditions, including healthy input, are
reported in main-paper Table III.

Under stale input, all 122 $\pi_1$ area exits occur after throw resolution;
121 follow a dodge. Its 11 exits under blank input are also late. For $\pi_2$,
stale input produces seven exits at or before resolution and three afterward.
Its two exits under blank input are late. These timings describe when
area-compliance failures occur; they do not explain the learned policies.

\subsection{Original fresh-image restoration experiment}
For $\pi_1$, restoration versus continuous stale input changes success from
32/64 to 61/64 (+45.3 percentage points; paired 95\% confidence interval
+32.8 to +57.8). There are 29 trials changing from failure to success and none
changing from success to failure. For $\pi_2$, success changes from 53/64 to
51/64 ($-$3.1 percentage points; confidence interval $-$7.8 to 0), with no
trials changing from failure to success and two changing from success to
failure. Both $\pi_1$ conditions dodge 64/64 throws, and both $\pi_2$ conditions
dodge 54/64. All five conditions have zero falls and aborted throws.
Main-paper Table II gives all counts, including the smaller or absent effects
of restoration after blank input. These results concern area compliance after
the encounter and establish neither a neural mechanism nor a universal fallback.

\subsection{Area radius, duration, and continuous distance}
Post-hoc analyses use area radii 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.25, 1.5, and 2.0 m and
observation durations 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, and 4.0 s in the original restoration and
shared-throw cohorts. Increases in success rate emerge mainly late and shrink
with larger area radii. At four seconds, increasing the radius from 1 to 1.5 m
reduces the additional successful $\pi_1$ trials from 29/64 to 2/64 in the
original cohort and from 37/64 to 1/64 in the shared-throw control.

Pelvis distance is horizontal distance from its starting position. Maximum
pelvis distance is its largest value during the trial; final pelvis distance
is its value at trial end. These summaries include all 64 seeds per policy in
each cohort, including hits, and reuse recorded trials. They do not apply an
area criterion.

For $\pi_1$, restoration reduces mean maximum pelvis distance from 1.043 to
0.860 m (paired difference $-$0.184 m; 95\% confidence interval $-$0.226 to
$-$0.146 m). The corresponding shared-throw difference is $-$0.179 m.
Mean final pelvis distance changes by $-$0.608 m in the original cohort and
$-$0.581 m in the shared-throw cohort.

For $\pi_2$, the mean maximum pelvis distance changes by $+0.010$ m in the
original cohort (paired 95\% confidence interval $+0.005$ to $+0.018$ m) and
$-0.002$ m under shared throws (interval $-0.019$ to $+0.012$ m). The latter
interval spans zero. Mean final pelvis distance changes by $-0.070$ and
$-0.060$ m, respectively. Thus, final distance can decrease without a
corresponding decrease in maximum distance.

\subsection{Stale-to-blank control}
The control reruns seeds 9000--9063 for both policies in three conditions,
giving 384 trials. All conditions receive fresh frames before 1.16 s, then
repeat the last pre-fault frame. At the fixed 2 s switch, they continue stale
input, supply blank input, or resume fresh frames. Histories keep updating.
All 128 triplets pass launch-state, throw-record, supplied-frame hash checks
before 2 s, and recorded pelvis-trace checks through 2 s. Native camera frames
at the switch also match before substitution. All throws resolve by the switch
within numerical tolerance. Every condition retains every seed through the
four-second horizon.

For $\pi_1$, success is 31/64 with continued stale input, 62/64 with blank input,
and 62/64 with fresh frames. The two switches change the same 31 trials from
failure to success, with none worsening. Each success-rate increase is
$31/64=48.4$ percentage points (paired 95\% confidence interval 35.9--60.9).
For $\pi_2$, all three conditions succeed on the same 54/64 trials. All $\pi_1$
trials dodge; each $\pi_2$ condition dodges 55/64. No falls or aborted throws
occur.

Fresh-image restoration versus blank input yields zero discordant success
pairs in 64 trials per policy. Under independent evaluation-seed sampling,
the one-sided exact 95\% upper bound on discordance probability $p$ follows
from $(1-p)^{64}=0.05$, giving $p\leq1-0.05^{1/64}=0.0457$, or 4.6\% rounded.
These are separate per-policy bounds. The empirical paired bootstrap has zero
width for these identical binary outcomes, so it cannot establish a population
equivalence margin.

Fresh frames reduce mean final pelvis distance relative to blank input by
0.295 m for $\pi_1$ (paired 95\% confidence interval 0.253--0.341 m) and
0.117 m for $\pi_2$ (0.091--0.144 m). The fresh-minus-blank difference in mean
maximum pelvis distance is $+0.0015$ m for $\pi_1$ (interval $-0.0008$ to
$+0.0039$ m) and $-0.0046$ m for $\pi_2$ ($-0.0124$ to $+0.0040$ m).
Blank input with retained history reproduces the observed success outcomes,
while fresh frames produce smaller mean final distances.

The simulator, robot, and weight files match the frozen runtime manifests.
The local rerun uses Python 3.12.3, NumPy 2.2.6, MuJoCo 3.3.7, ONNX Runtime
1.23.2 CPU inference with one thread per session, and an Intel Core Ultra
9 275HX. Trajectories differ from the published archives: continued-stale/fresh
success changes from archived 32/61 to local 31/62 for $\pi_1$, and from 53/51
to 54/54 for $\pi_2$, all out of 64. Every reported switch comparison uses the
local triplets. The cause of the cross-runtime differences remains unresolved.
This exploratory control reuses the original restoration seed IDs and is
analysed separately.
